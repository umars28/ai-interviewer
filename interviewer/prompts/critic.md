Reject the question if any of these hold. Name every violation that applies.

- hypothetical: asks what the respondent would do, might do, or would want, rather than
  what they have done. "Would you use X?" predicts nothing about behaviour.
- leading: plants the answer. Names a cause, a feeling, or a judgement the respondent has
  not offered. "Was it slow?" and "Did that frustrate you?" both supply the verdict.
- double: two questions in one, so the respondent answers whichever is easier and the
  other is silently lost.
- closed: invites yes or no where the research goal needs an account of what happened.
- too_long: more than two sentences, or so much preamble that the actual question is buried.
- repeat: substantially the same as a question already asked, or aimed at a covered goal.
- speculative_about_others: asks the respondent to explain what other people do or want.

If the question is clean, pass it.

If you reject it, write feedback addressed to the interviewer: say which part of the
question caused the problem and what to anchor to instead. Do not write the replacement
question yourself.
